3What is running

Four things. One collects data every quarter hour, two are design concepts built to be looked at, and the fourth is the name the paid work goes out under.

3.1Aussie Health Utility

A collector that reads how many people are waiting in each NSW emergency department that publishes a live count1, every fifteen minutes, and appends the reading to a PostgreSQL series that is never rewritten. Fifty-nine departments publish such a count2. It has been collecting since 20 July 2026 and holds more than 120,000 readings.

The interval is not a preference. The upstream feed publishes the current moment only and keeps no history, so a reading not taken at the time is gone for good3. That is also why the early weeks have gaps and the later ones do not4. A dump of the database is taken at 03:30 Melbourne time each night and copied off the machine5.

No personal or health information is collected, because the schema has no fields for it6.

3.2Still Wild

A concept rebuild for a real Brisbane adventure business, written as one self-contained HTML file. It holds a live canvas dawn scene, a dictionary in five languages — English, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, Japanese and Korean — and an honesty layer that labels every invented detail as invented, on the page, where a reader will see it.

Photographs are used with the business's permission, for prototyping. The concept is not affiliated with them8. It is live at lilliss.au/stillwild-concept/.

3.3The Span

A design concept: one screen holding 1975 to 2117, with the space programme of the United Arab Emirates drawn as a single span you travel by dragging. Fifteen milestones sit on it. Each is a drawn line that means its own event rather than an icon taken off a shelf, and each names the source that establishes it; thirteen link straight to the government page they came from. Nothing on it is estimated9.

It is deep-linkable to any year, navigable by keyboard, and with scripting switched off it reads as a plain sourced list. It is live at lilliss.au/xperience-concept/spine.html.

5How these pages are built

Every page on this site is a single HTML file. There is no build step and no framework. There are zero external requests: the fonts are self-hosted and nothing is loaded from another host, so a page keeps working with the network cable pulled once it has loaded.

  • One file per page, served as it was written.
  • One typeface on this page, Inter, in one weight range, at five sizes.
  • One accent, used only for links.
  • Data fetched from this origin only, and only where the page still reads correctly without it.

Set in Inter, self-hosted, one file. Five sizes: 12.8, 16, 20, 25 and 31.25 pixels, each a quarter larger than the one below it. Footnote markers are set at the smallest of the five and raised, rather than scaled to a sixth size. Small capitals are synthesised from Inter at 500 weight with the tracking opened by a tenth of an em, and are used only for labels.

The measure is 36 rem. Body text is set 16 on 26. Every leading and every gap between blocks is 13 pixels or a plain multiple of it, down to a half: 19.5 is a unit and a half, and a single-line table row measures 39, which is three. Where a control needs a 44-pixel target, its padding leaves the grid to reach it. A section heading carries six units of space above it — four before the rule and two after it — and one unit below, which is why a section reads as belonging to its heading rather than to the paragraph before. Section and note numbers hang in the left margin so the text edge never breaks. Figures are lining and tabular, so a column of numbers aligns on the left instead of being right-aligned to fake it.

Two colours of text on two grounds, one link colour, and nothing else. Contrast on the light ground: body 15.8:1, secondary 6.6:1, links 8.4:1. On the dark ground: body 14.6:1, secondary 7.1:1, links 8.3:1. Rules are drawn at no less than 3.1:1, in either theme and on either ground.
