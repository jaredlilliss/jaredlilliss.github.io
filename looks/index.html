One descent

Not three designs any more — one, with two rooms that open. Start at the top and go down; two of the eight floors are doors.

Not live. The site itself is unchanged.

Start here

  1. 01The Section

    A cut downward through the layers the work actually touches, from the terminal on a counter to the row in a database and back up to a page anyone can read. Depth is just the scroll, which is why it works on a phone with nothing switched off.

The two rooms that open

Reachable from inside the descent. Each is also a real page on its own, and each knows the way back to the floor you left.

  1. L1The Rail

    Off the counter. The same claim — trading since 2019 — laid along a line you drag through time. Desktop only, on purpose: on a phone it becomes a plain readable list of the same seven stops rather than pretending.

  2. L6Every Reading

    Off the row. Literally every row the collector has ever written — 59 emergency departments down, every day across, in a hand-written shader. Deliberately heavy and built for a mouse, which is exactly why it sits two clicks deep instead of at the front door. It reads its numbers live, so nothing on it can go stale, and where the collector was not running the material is simply not there. If a machine cannot run it, it says so and draws the flat version.

A study, not a candidate

  1. 間Ma

    Built after measuring an award-winning site against my own with the same instrument. Its type ran 63× from largest to smallest; mine ran five to eight, with everything between 10px and 50px. I had no dynamic range — every page was the same volume throughout. So this one has no ground canvas, no translucent panels, no tracked mono labels and no thin line marks: the four habits that had quietly become a template. What carries it instead is scale, emptiness and timing. Ma is the interval — negative space as an active element rather than what is left over.
